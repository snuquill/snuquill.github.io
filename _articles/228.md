Dear Readers,

Spring has come to renew the calendar; with it, a new edition.

I myself was new to the position of editor-in-chief not too long ago. There certainly was a fair amount of tension during those first days. The initial tasks that I had to get through after taking up the mantle felt like insurmountable mountains. Upon reflection as I write these words, those same tasks seem like small details in the larger picture; the base sketch of the final painting that is managing the creation of a new magazine.

Just as I perceive tasks differently from when I was at the beginning of my job as opposed to the end, we perceive the world in our own various ways. It is a lesson learned in elementary school, to know and understand that everyone can have different views regarding any topic. Nevertheless, it is still an important piece of wisdom that continues to be relevant, and sometimes we all need a reminder of the most basic of rules. I imagine I will, as I walk into my second semester as editor-in-chief and face the same duties again. The SNU Quill thus strives to bring you varied perspectives of varied issues within the SNU community, but it also continually works to bring you new angles and explore topics that may seem like a given.

Hence our 85th theme, Frame. A frame is the most basic of structures, on which foundations are built. Frame is the structure through which we view our surroundings. Frame is a choice that we make, conscious or unconscious, as we navigate our rapidly changing world. It is also the threshold of new spaces, a border through which we can look or step.

The 83rd and 84th editions looked to the past and future, respectively. This edition attempts to look at the present alongside both, painting pictures of the various events that have existed, currently persist, and will continue to remain throughout the passage of time with a careful brush.

SNU Society first brings to you a series of snapshots, capturing stories of the campus. We are invited to consider the issue of housing, where students are weighing their priorities as well as tangible and intangible costs. From a long-standing topic we turn to a more recent shift: the Office of Global Affairs (OGA) to Office of International Affairs (OIA) name change and what sort of message it sends with its changes, external and internal. Continuing the topic of SNU’s internationality, we then explore the various reasons SNU students choose to go on the long journey of an exchange student, as well as the intersections within these categories.

In Features, we move back to view things from a broader perspective. We start in Korea with Konglish: what is commonly perceived as an infringement of external language on Korean takes a different meaning in the context of Korea’s history. From there, the view is expanded to a global level with the topic of climate justice in court, where we meet an attorney who weighs in on how climate harm is being translated into legal responsibility worldwide. We circle back to Korea but do not lose connection to the globe or history; the modern issue of cat moms versus environmentalists is shown to be deeply connected to the feminists and bird conservationists of the late 19th century.

Finally, in Arts and Culture, readers are presented with fascinating perspectives on media and culture that can be taken for granted. Omori explores the layers of grief and repression of trauma in what seems like just a simple horror game at a baseline. There are the fast food fads that have taken over Korea as well as the rest of the world, but keep getting shorter with each turnaround; and a more long-lasting trend, MBTI, which has allowed Koreans to define each other for perhaps half a decade — although you will soon see that this categorization of the self is nothing new.

All in all, these are the snapshots of this edition, carefully rendered by each writer. While reading the stories on display in this edition, I encourage and invite you to think about your own frames — and how they define how you view the world. Welcome to the 85th edition.

Sincerely yours,
Soomin Chae
